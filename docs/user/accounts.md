## Start from a ready chart {#starter-charts}

A new Book has no accounts. Agents create them as they record your statements, using the names an ordinary bookkeeper would use. If you'd rather start with a full list, open **Accounts** and choose **Use Business chart** or **Use Personal chart**. The one that matches your Book comes first.

A starter chart only adds ordinary accounts, such as Checking, Credit card, Sales, Rent and Groceries, grouped under headings such as Assets and Operating expenses. Headings take no postings of their own; entries go to the accounts beneath them. You can rename, regroup or retire any of them afterwards. A chart can be added only while the Book has no accounts, and Vult records which chart and version it came from.

## Group accounts under a parent {#parents}

A parent groups accounts on the Accounts page and in reports: Checking and Savings can sit under Bank accounts, and reports add them into it. Grouping never moves money or changes a balance.

To set one, open the account, choose the **⋯** menu and **Set parent account**. A parent is an account of the same type, active, in the same Book. The chart can be up to four levels deep, and an account can't sit under itself or one of its own children.

Vult never guesses parents from names. An account called "Expenses:Travel" stays where it is until you or an agent give it a parent.

## Retire an account {#retire}

Retire an account you no longer use, such as a closed savings account. A retired account takes no new entries. Its history stays in every report, and you can reactivate it later from the same menu.

An account must be empty when it retires. If it still holds money, Vult asks where it went: "This account still holds $40.00. Where did it go?" Choose the account that received it and the date it moved. Vult records an ordinary transfer of the exact remaining balance and the retirement together, so you never end up with one without the other. For an account kept in another currency, the transfer moves the amount in that currency too.

## Account roles {#roles}

An account's role says what it is for, and it must fit the account's type:

| Role | Type | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash | Asset | Bank, savings or cash on hand |
| Receivable | Asset | Money owed to you |
| Fixed asset | Asset | Equipment or property |
| Investment | Asset | Investments that aren't cash |
| Card | Liability | A credit card |
| Loan | Liability | Borrowing |
| Payable | Liability | Money you owe |
| Cost of sales | Expense | The direct cost of what you sold; reports show gross profit |

Vult uses roles, never account names, to decide what counts as cash.

## What agents can do {#agents}

An agent with write access can set parents, retire and reactivate accounts, and add a starter chart. An agent that proposes prepares the same changes for you in **Approvals**, with the retirement's transfer shown line by line. A read-only agent sees each account's parent and status.
