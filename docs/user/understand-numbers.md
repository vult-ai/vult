## One period for every figure {#choose-a-period}

The Dashboard's date range controls every figure and chart on the page. Both the start and end dates are included. Figures use each entry's accounting date. While a new period loads, Vult keeps showing the previous figures until the new ones are ready.

## Cash {#cash-balance}

Cash is the recorded balance of the accounts marked as cash, at the end of the period. It includes all earlier history, so changing only the start date doesn't change it. It shows what the Book records, not a live bank balance. Vult doesn't connect to your bank.

## Cash flow {#cash-flow}

The Cash flow statement shows how cash moved in the period, from opening cash to closing cash. Cash is every account marked as cash. Each movement is sorted by the account on the other side of its entry:

- **Financing:** money from or to owners (equity accounts) and loans.
- **Investing:** buying or selling equipment, property and investments.
- **Operating:** everything else, including income, spending and paying a credit card.
- **Transfers between your own cash accounts** are left out; they add up to zero.
- **Opening balances** are where cash starts, not money coming in. An entry against the account with the Opening balances role becomes the period's opening cash. The starter charts give their Opening balances account that role.

When one entry mixes sections in a way Vult can't split exactly, its amount shows as **Unclassified** rather than being guessed. Opening cash plus every section always equals closing cash.


## Income, expenses and net result {#profit}

Income and expenses are the amounts posted to income and expense accounts during the period. Net result is income minus expenses. For example, $1,000 of income and $300 of expenses give a net result of $700.

Money that enters the business isn't automatically income. A loan or an owner's contribution changes cash without changing the net result. Refunds and credits appear as reductions rather than disappearing.

## Where the money went {#where-the-money-went}

This panel ranks the period's biggest expense or income accounts. Switch between money in and money out. On the income and expenses chart, **Show table** lists the exact amounts by period and account.

## Statements {#statements}

**Statements** gives you the Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss, Trial Balance, General Ledger and Journal for any date or period, with comparisons such as previous month or previous year. Each report states its basis and coverage, so you know what it rests on. Select an amount to see the entries behind it, and download any report as CSV.

Statements reflect what has been recorded. They are not audited, and they are not a tax calculation.

## When something looks wrong {#needs-attention}

The Accounts page flags balances worth a closer look, such as a cash account that is overdrawn or a card paid past what was owed. Open its activity to investigate. A surprising balance usually means a missing or miscategorized entry. Ask your agent to find it, then reverse and re-record it if needed.
